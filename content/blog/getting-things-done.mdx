When we're younger we rarely stop to consider the long-term consequences of our actions. We do things because we want to and worry about the effects later. But as we get older our decisions begin to shape our futures, our ambitions demand more of us, and the distance between what we imagine and what we can actually accomplish becomes harder to ignore.

So, how do things actually get done?

"Things" in this context is used to imply any ambition one might be working torwads, from as little as repeating a daily habit or organising an event to things like starting a startup or becoming a great partner. These ambitions may differ in scale, but they all raise an interesting question: what does it take to turn an intention into a reality?

It seems to me that getting things done involves a combination of immerse focus, personal connection to what we're doing and the people we choose to do it with.

**What you get done**

It's extremely hard to get things done when you work on what you're not interested in. Naturally, people tend to work harder and have an affinity towards things they are fascinated by or outcomes that matter to them. When we care about something, we're more willing to invest the time and effort required to understand even the ugly parts of it.

For example Someone might be motivated to finish a project because they want a good grade. Someone else might work on the same project because they're fascinated by the problem. Both people might finish it, but their relationships with the work could be very different.

The second person might be more willing to investigate unexpected problems, learn unfamiliar skills, or continue after the excitement has finished.

**Who you get it done with**

doing hard things requires a great team. It's extremely important to find smart, productive, postive and happy people that do not belittle your ambition.These are people who will encourage you when things become difficult but also challenge your thinking when they believe you're wrong.

Because such expetional people are rare, when you find them, invest in them. When you find a great Co-founder, treat them with respect and make them feel heard. When you find a great friend, prioritize the relationship. When you find a great spouse, out-love them each day.

Get good at indefying what people are good at and delegate work that way. For example, imagine you're organising an event. One person is excellent at coming up with new ideas, another is meticulous about logistics, and a third is comfortable speaking to sponsors. Assigning responsibilities based on those strengths could make the event easier to organise.

Well, But what happens when someone wants to do something outside their strengths? Even though it might slow down progress initially, it would help them grow right? Should we then organise people according to what they already do well or should we create an envroinment that allows them to grow?

I thnk the response to this would vary contextually. If you're trying to meet a deadline, assigning work according to existing strengths might be sensible. If you're working on a long-term goal, helping people develop new skills along the way might be just as important.

**Focus**

Focus is more than concentrating on the task in front of you. I would use three words to describe it; Attention, Commitment and priotization

There are always more projects to start, skills to learn, and opportunities to explore. The difficulty is not necessarily finding something worthwhile to do, but deciding which things we should actually commit ourselves to.

Being busy can give us the satisfaction of making progress without necessarily bringing us closer to what we want to achieve. Sometimes, getting things done requires resisting the temptation to pursue every opportunity and concentrating on the few that matter most.

Ultimately, getting things done is about discovering what matters to you, having the discipline to give it your full attention, and surrounding yourself with people who help you become better. There is no universal formula, but these are a good place to start from.
